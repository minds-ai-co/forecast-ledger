## Scorecard

- **Pairs right:** 64 of 108 (59%; a coin flip is 50%)
- **Rank correlation:** 0.21 (Spearman, forecast score vs days in Popular; not statistically meaningful with 16 stories)
- **Top 3 hit:** 2 of 3 (Mindfulness and Anesthesia; missed the Applebaum foreign-policy piece)

A modest first pass. The Minds put two of the three longest Popular runs in their top 3 and ordered 59% of story pairs correctly, but the rank correlation is weak. They undersold the Applebaum piece and the WNBA story, ranked the AI pandemic story 2nd although it never reached Popular, and scores sit in a narrow 4.9 to 6.5 band. This is the starting point a real pilot would improve on.

## Method

- **Question per story:** Headline, subhead and lead image as shown on the homepage. How likely are you to click and read this story? 0 to 10.
- **Forecast score:** Mean rating from the individual Minds answers (26 to 28 of 30 Minds answered each story). Top box is the share answering 8 to 10.
- **Real outcome:** Days a story appeared in the homepage Popular list (top 10), from 130 Wayback snapshots of theatlantic.com on 55 days, 20 August to 18 November 2026. Snapshot counts and best rank break ties.
- **Stories:** 16 pieces first seen on the homepage 21 August to 13 October 2026: 13 that reached Popular and 3 that were featured on the homepage but never did.
- **Scoring:** Pairs right counts every pair of stories with different outcomes and asks whether the forecast ordered them the same way, as on the public forecast ledger.
- **Forecast date:** 20 November 2026.

## Audience

The Atlantic Readers (US), 30 Minds. Qualification: reads The Atlantic at least monthly (19) or is a paid subscriber (11). Market: United States, English. Grounding: Pew Research Center, Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025 (US), American Press Institute 2026 media habits, and The Atlantic's published subscriber figures.
